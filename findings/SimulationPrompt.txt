# Parasite Eve Recomp: Phase 3
## Fully Integrated Weapon Progression, BP, Loot, Donor Equipment, and Combat Balance Simulation

### Mission

Continue the Parasite Eve Recomp weapon-balancing project from the completed October 17 Phase 1 and Phase 2 investigations.

Do not restart the research or discard the existing simulation framework.

Our previous investigations established that the weapon-effect rebalance has improved many underpowered modifications, but the P90, MAG, and other weapons still differ substantially in practical performance. We also identified potential shotgun weaknesses involving magazine capacity, attack commitment, and close-range reward.

However, a major limitation remains in our simulations:

**We have been comparing weapons using simplified, predetermined upgrade budgets instead of modeling how players actually develop their equipment throughout the game.**

In Parasite Eve, weapon progression comes from several interconnected systems:

1. Bonus Points (BP) earned through gameplay and spent on weapon stats, armor stats, Active Time, or Item Capacity.
2. Transferable Attack, Range, and Bullet bonuses obtained from unwanted weapons.
3. Tools and Super Tools used to transfer equipment statistics and modifications.
4. Rare-drop rewards and stat upgrades from our Recomp loot cascade.
5. Fixed equipment pickups, enemy drops, purchases, and optional rewards.
6. Modifications, Mod Permits, the Mod Catalog, rebuilding, and other customization systems.
7. Inventory and storage limitations.
8. Character leveling and combat progression.

All these systems interact.

**Your assignment is to build a fully integrated, stateful, resource-constrained progression simulator, connect it to the validated combat simulator, and reevaluate weapon balance using equipment that players can realistically obtain and upgrade.**

Work autonomously through source inspection, implementation, testing, Monte Carlo experiments, documentation, and recommendations.

Do not pause for approval between research phases.

You may change simulator code and tests, but do not implement changes to the actual game's combat, loot, BP, or equipment balance without review.

---

# 1. Core design philosophy

Preserve the appeal of Parasite Eve's weapon-customization system.

I want players to feel rewarded for:

- Collecting equipment.
- Finding rare upgrades.
- Extracting useful stats from unwanted weapons.
- Spending BP intelligently.
- Choosing whether to improve Aya or her equipment.
- Experimenting with different weapons and modifications.
- Building and maintaining a favorite weapon.
- Making meaningful tradeoffs between combat efficiency and flexibility.

Do not automatically recommend increasing a weapon's base stats when players already have reasonable ways to improve them.

For example, the M10B shotgun has a seven-round magazine.

Phase 2 proposed increasing it to eight rounds because one selected build fires eight bullets per turn.

But a player may be able to obtain that extra capacity through BP investment or transferring a Bullet bonus from another weapon.

**We must establish the actual cost, availability, and opportunity cost of that upgrade before deciding whether the M10B needs a permanent buff.**

The same principle applies to Attack, Range, and other equipment parameters.

The objective is not equal weapon DPS. It is meaningful progression and viable build diversity under realistic resource constraints.

# 5. Phase Two: Reconstruct the complete BP system

BP must be modeled as a first-class progression resource.

It cannot be represented as a fixed number of free weapon upgrades per story day.

## Verify BP acquisition

Inspect the actual game logic for:

- BP earned from combat.
- BP awarded through leveling.
- BP awarded through progression or completion.
- BP modifiers or penalties involving battle performance.
- Whether incoming damage affects BP rewards.
- Whether BP can be earned through repeatable encounters.
- Whether unused BP carries forward.
- BP persistence across EX Game or repeated playthroughs.

Do not assume a fixed BP reward per battle.

Verify the actual equations, including rounding and caps.

Where sources suggest a standard cost of 100 BP per improvement, confirm that against the game code and current Recomp implementation rather than treating it as universally authoritative.

## Verify BP spending

Determine the actual behavior and cost of improving:

- Weapon Attack.
- Weapon Range.
- Weapon Bullet capacity.
- Armor statistics.
- Active Time.
- Item Capacity.

Check all available stat categories and restrictions.

For each improvement establish:

- BP cost.
- Increase granted.
- Current-value dependencies.
- Maximum level or stat cap.
- Eligibility requirements.
- Whether the cost increases.
- Whether the improvement is permanent.
- Whether equipment BP improvements can later be transferred using Tools.

This last point is particularly important.

**BP-derived weapon bonuses may become part of the transferable stat pool. Confirm that behavior rather than double-counting or losing them during simulated transfers.**

## Persistent BP ledger

Each playthrough must track:

- BP earned.
- BP available.
- BP spent.
- BP invested in Attack.
- BP invested in Range.
- BP invested in Bullet capacity.
- BP invested in armor.
- BP invested in Active Time.
- BP invested in Item Capacity.
- Unspent BP.
- BP investment history.

Every BP transaction must have a source and destination.

Never permit BP duplication, overspending, or impossible stat increases.

# 6. Phase Three: Model BP opportunity costs

BP competes across fundamentally different investments.

The simulator must account for that.

For example, spending BP on a shotgun's Bullet stat might improve its efficiency by aligning the magazine with an eight-bullet volley.

But the same BP could instead improve:

- Attack damage.
- Effective weapon range.
- Aya's Active Time.
- Carrying capacity.
- Defensive equipment.

These investments have different short-term and long-term value.

## Active Time

Model actual changes to ATB filling using the verified formula.

An Active Time upgrade may benefit every weapon Aya equips, while a weapon-specific upgrade benefits only one equipment item unless transferred.

This can change the economics of multi-weapon strategies.

## Item Capacity

Increasing carrying capacity may allow players to:

- Pick up additional donor weapons.
- Carry additional Tools.
- Avoid discarding valuable resources.
- Retain a second specialized weapon.
- Keep more healing items or ammunition where relevant.

Do not assign Item Capacity a fictional direct DPS bonus.

Its value comes from actual inventory decisions and future resource opportunities.

## Armor

Defensive improvements may reduce damage taken, improve battle success, or affect BP earnings where the game's reward formula supports that connection.

Do not assume every point of defense has the same value in every encounter.

## BP strategy experiments

Test:

1. Weapon-stat-focused investment.
2. Active-Time-first investment.
3. Item-Capacity-first investment.
4. Defensive-equipment-focused investment.
5. Balanced investment.
6. Saving BP for a later weapon.
7. Adaptive investment based on current resources.

Use the same BP earnings and acquisition opportunities when comparing policies.

Do not allow an optimizing policy to know future random drops.

# 9. Phase Six: Model donor weapon transfers

This is central to the new simulator.

An unwanted weapon is not merely junk. It can be a source of transferable statistics or effects.

## Weapon ownership

Every weapon should exist as an individual inventory object.

Track:

- Unique instance ID.
- Base weapon type.
- Intrinsic stats.
- Current transferable bonuses.
- BP-derived bonuses.
- Transferred donor bonuses.
- Other applied stat improvements.
- Installed effects.
- Acquisition source.
- Transfer history.
- Current storage location.

## Stat provenance

Keep an explicit record of the origin of every bonus point.

This is necessary for auditing the system and preventing duplicate resources.

However, do not invent additional gameplay restrictions based on provenance if the game treats all transferable bonuses identically.

## Transfer mechanics

Inspect the actual transfer code.

Verify:

- Tool cost.
- Super Tool cost and differences.
- What transfers.
- Whether all three weapon stat bonuses transfer together.
- How modifications transfer.
- What happens when destination caps are reached.
- How the source weapon changes.
- Whether the source remains available.
- What happens if there is no modification slot.
- Whether stat and effect transfers share the same action or require separate actions.

Avoid approximating a Tool as a generic currency that can improve any stat.

A Tool can only produce benefits that the actual transfer rules permit.

## Prevent duplication

Never allow:

- Transferring intrinsic base stats as bonuses.
- Extracting the same donor bonuses repeatedly.
- Creating an item with impossible stats.
- Reusing a consumed Tool.
- Applying a transferred BP bonus twice.
- Exceeding verified stat caps.

## Donor-selection behavior

Simulate decisions about whether to:

- Extract a donor immediately.
- Keep it for later.
- Use it as a replacement weapon.
- Transfer its effects.
- Store it.
- Sell or discard it.
- Save a Tool for a better donor.

A player may have many donors but few Tools.

Determine which resources are actually limiting progression.

# 10. Phase Seven: Model direct stat upgrade rewards

Separate three sources of weapon stat growth:

**BP investments:** Purchased with earned BP.

**Donor bonuses:** Obtained from equipment and transferred using Tools.

**Rare/stat reward upgrades:** Obtained through loot or other rewards and applied according to their own rules.

Do not merge these into a generic pool of free weapon points.

Track their provenance in the final weapon while preserving the game's actual transfer mechanics.

For every upgrade, identify:

- Stat affected.
- Amount gained.
- Item or currency consumed.
- Eligibility rules.
- Time obtained.
- Equipment receiving the upgrade.
- Transferability afterward.

This makes it possible to determine whether the loot overhaul or BP system contributes more to player power.

# 12. Phase Nine: Simulate continuous playthroughs

Replace fixed daily budgets with actual accumulated progression.

Each simulated playthrough should:

1. Begin with appropriate initial state.
2. Enter encounters in a plausible progression sequence.
3. Simulate battle outcomes.
4. Calculate XP and BP rewards.
5. Generate equipment and rare drops.
6. Update pity and inventory.
7. Handle inventory overflow.
8. Process item acquisition.
9. Apply player policy decisions.
10. Spend BP when appropriate.
11. Use Tools for donor transfers.
12. Install modifications when available.
13. Upgrade or change equipped weapons.
14. Continue to the next checkpoint.

Do not award an arbitrary "+12 Attack" because the player reached Day 5.

Every improvement must have an actual source.

### Prevent circular simulation shortcuts

Combat performance affects progression through battle outcomes, resource use, and potentially BP.

Progression also changes future combat performance.

Implement this feedback explicitly where feasible.

If detailed combat simulation of every encounter becomes too expensive, use a validated surrogate model for routine encounters.

Evaluate representative combats using the full engine at checkpoints.

Document where approximation is used.

# 14. Phase Eleven: Player investment policies

Test distinct player behaviors.

### Policy A: Casual

Uses equipment naturally, spends some BP, transfers bonuses occasionally, and does not perfectly optimize.

### Policy B: Favorite-weapon specialist

Chooses a preferred weapon class, invests heavily in it, and extracts donor bonuses whenever worthwhile.

### Policy C: Weapon optimizer

Uses BP, Tools, donors, and modifications strategically to maximize combat effectiveness.

### Policy D: Two-weapon specialist

Maintains a primary and secondary weapon with distinct encounter roles.

### Policy E: Flexible experimenter

Frequently changes equipment, relying on lossless transfers and the Mod Catalog to explore builds.

### Policy F: Farmer

Acquires extra BP, Tools, and donors through repeatable encounters.

### Policy G: ATB-focused

Prioritizes Active Time before investing heavily in weapon statistics.

### Policy H: Inventory-focused

Invests in Item Capacity to preserve more donor equipment and useful resources.

### Policy I: Balanced progression

Makes moderate investments across BP categories.

These policies can be combined with exploration profiles.

Do not run an unnecessarily large Cartesian product if many combinations are redundant.

Use a representative experiment design and expand only where differences are meaningful.

**Route A: BP investment**

**Route B: Donor transfer**

**Route C: Rare reward**

# Final objective

I want a realistic picture of what weapon customization looks like over an entire Parasite Eve playthrough.

The system should not assume players acquire a fixed number of weapon upgrades or receive all their improvements automatically at specific story days.

It should simulate the decisions players actually make:

- Should I spend BP on faster ATB?
- Should I increase my inventory capacity to collect more donors?
- Should I improve my current weapon's Attack?
- Should I invest in Bullet capacity to support my modifications?
- Should I save BP for later?
- Should I use a Tool now or wait for a better donor?
- Should I maintain two weapons or concentrate all resources in one?
- Should I change to a newly acquired weapon or keep improving my favorite?
- Does a rare drop change my best strategy?

All of those decisions compete for finite resources.

**Every improvement must be earned, acquired, transferred, or purchased through a verified progression mechanic.**

The result should tell us whether our existing customization systems already solve the apparent weapon balance problems.

Only after that should we decide whether the M10B, P90, MAG, or any other weapon needs gameplay changes.
